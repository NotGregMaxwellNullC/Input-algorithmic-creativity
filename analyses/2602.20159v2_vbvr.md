## Summary

Rapid progress in video models has focused on visual quality, leaving reasoning
capabilities underexplored. Video reasoning grounds intelligence in
spatiotemporally consistent visual environments beyond what text captures,
enabling intuitive reasoning over continuity, interaction, and causality.

The authors introduce the Very Big Video Reasoning (VBVR) Dataset — an
unprecedentedly large-scale resource spanning **200 curated reasoning tasks**
following a principled taxonomy and **over 1 million video clips** (~3 orders of
magnitude larger than existing datasets). They also present **VBVR-Bench**, a
verifiable evaluation framework using rule-based, human-aligned scorers for
reproducible and interpretable diagnosis of video reasoning.

## Cognitive Framework (5 Pillars)

The taxonomy is grounded in philosophical, cognitive, and neuroscientific
literature (Newell & Simon, 1972; Anderson, 2007):

1. **Abstraction** — reasoning about abstract patterns and relationships
2. **Knowledge** — applying world knowledge and commonsense
3. **Perception** — fine-grained visual and auditory understanding
4. **Spatiality** — spatial reasoning about 3D structure and layout
5. **Transformation** — temporal reasoning about change and causality

## Key Results

- **VBVR-Wan2.2** (fine-tuned open-source) achieves overall score **0.685** —
  an 84.6% relative improvement over its base model
- Fine-tuned open-source model **outperforms all closed commercial models**
  including Sora 2 and Veo 3.1 on reasoning tasks
- Human performance: **0.974** — a considerable gap remains
- Persistent challenges in long-horizon temporal reasoning and robust symbolic
  manipulation

## Scaling Behavior

- **0 → 200K training examples**: substantial gain (0.412 → 0.767 on in-domain)
- After 200K: curve plateaus and begins to fluctuate
- Both in-domain and out-of-domain scores improve together → genuine
  generalization, not memorization
- Early signs of **emergent generalization** to unseen reasoning tasks

## Relevance to Algorithmic Creativity

This paper is highly relevant to the study of algorithmic creativity because:

- It provides a **principled cognitive taxonomy** for reasoning that maps to
  human cognitive architecture — directly applicable to understanding creative
  reasoning in AI systems
- The **Abstraction** and **Transformation** pillars capture core aspects of
  creative thought: pattern recognition, analogy, and novel recombination
- Evidence of **emergent generalization** to unseen tasks suggests that
  sufficiently scaled reasoning training may yield creative transfer
- The finding that open-source fine-tuned models can surpass proprietary ones
  on reasoning tasks highlights the democratization of AI reasoning capabilities
